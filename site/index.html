Madden–Julian Oscillation events from the RMM index (1979–present) and Large-scale Precipitation Tracking (Jan 1998 – Aug 2026)

Nirmal Mathew Alex · Florida Institute of Technology

Summary. The Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO) is the dominant mode of tropical intraseasonal variability: a planetary-scale envelope of enhanced rainfall that travels eastward along the equator, returning every 30–60 days. This archive sets two ways of following it side by side — the Real-time Multivariate MJO index (RMM; Wheeler & Hendon 2004), a daily index of upper- and lower-level wind and cloudiness, and Large-scale Precipitation Tracking (LPT; Kerns & Chen 2016, 2020), which follows the rain systems themselves. Longitude–time (Hovmöller) diagram

Longitude–time diagram of the window; time increases downward, so eastward propagation runs down and to the right. LPT lines are centroid tracks of tracked rain systems (available ; click one for details). RMM is an index, not a location: its active days (amplitude ≥ 1) are placed at the approximate longitude of their phase, from the Wheeler & Hendon (2004) composites. The dotted line is a 5 m s⁻¹ eastward reference.

RMM phase diagram

(RMM1, RMM2) phase space after Wheeler & Hendon (2004). Counter-clockwise motion is eastward propagation; numbers are the eight MJO phases, labelled with where enhanced convection sits. Inside the unit circle (amplitude < 1) the MJO is weak. Colour runs with time; small labelled dots mark the 1st of each month.
